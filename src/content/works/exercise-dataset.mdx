An open fitness dataset built around one question the usual exercise dumps can't
answer: *how much did that set actually train each muscle?*

## What's in it

- **1,324 exercises**, one JSON file plus a JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) they
  validate against.
- **Per-muscle volume** on 1,115 of them (84%) — each muscle marked `1.0`
  (direct, a prime mover) or `0.5` (indirect synergist), keyed by a 22-muscle
  vocabulary shared with the `primary_muscle` / `secondary_muscle` labels.
- **A three-level movement hierarchy**: `body_region` → `movement_group` →
  `volume_profile`, i.e. 2 regions, 20 groups, 37 patterns.
- **A category on every record** — `strength` (1,201), `stretch` (69),
  `cardio` (34), `olympic` (20).
- **Instructions in 10 languages**, as both full text and ordered steps.

## Why profiles

Volume is attached to the movement pattern, not to the individual exercise. All
143 biceps-curl variants share one involvement map, so the classification is
reviewable in 37 decisions rather than 1,115 — and two exercises that are the
same movement can't quietly disagree about what they train.

The labels and the volume maps answer different questions on purpose:
`primary_muscle` forces a single winner and is good for browsing; `volume`
freely marks several muscles direct at once, because a bench press genuinely is
a direct chest, front-delt *and* triceps movement. Filter on the labels, count
sets with `volume`.

The 209 exercises left without volume — stretches, cardio, Olympic lifts,
isometric holds — are excluded deliberately rather than missing.

## Provenance

Names, equipment and the multilingual instructions derive from
[hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset](https://github.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset)
(MIT); its media files are separately licensed and were removed along with their
reference fields. The volume system — profiles, involvement maps, hierarchy and
muscle vocabulary — is this project's addition. Released under MIT.
